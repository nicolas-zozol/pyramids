## What the source tree actually holds

Read before anything else, because three documents describe this migration and none of them matches the code.

The corpus, fixed by the epic and re-confirmed by the story on 2026-07-29, is the 11 markdown files of `apps/robusta/content/blog`. Their slugs are not their file names: `validateFrontMatter` in `src/logic/posts.ts` derives the slug from the title through `immutableSlugify`, a function the file forbids changing in its first line. Derived slugs, to be regenerated by running the v1 code rather than trusted from this list:

- blockchain/ledger-versus-metamask.md — "Ledger versus Metamask" — `ledger-versus-metamask` — en
- blockchain/start-coding-blockchain.md — "Tooling for Solidity coders" — `tooling-for-solidity-coders` — en
- blockchain/yield-farming-fr.md — "Provenance des rendements du Yield Farming dans la blockchain" — `provenance-des-rendements-du-yield-farming-dans-la-blockchain` — fr
- blockchain/yield-farming.md — "The source of Yield Farming profits" — `the-source-of-yield-farming-profits` — declared fr, written in English
- javascript/pourquoi-migration-gatsby-next-js.md — "Pourquoi j'ai migré de Gatsby vers NextJS" — `pourquoi-jai-migre-de-gatsby-vers-nextjs` — fr
- javascript/styled-components.md — "Applying correctly className with Styled components" — `applying-correctly-classname-with-styled-components` — en
- javascript/typescript/completes-with.md — "Completing a RxJs Observable with another" — `completing-a-rxjs-observable-with-another` — en, categoryPath `javascript/typescript`
- javascript/why-migration-gatsby-next.md — "Why I made the migration from Gatsby toward NextJS" — `why-i-made-the-migration-from-gatsby-toward-nextjs` — declared fr, written in English

- web/easy-automation-with-sonoff.md — "Easy automation with Sonoff and Javascript" — `easy-automation-with-sonoff-and-javascript` — en, no author field

Six distinct category paths, not five: `javascript/typescript` is a nested category of its own, which the story's "javascript 4" count folds into its parent.

The v1 URL set is what `generateStaticParams` emits, and it contradicts `apps/robusta/README.md` on two points that decide the redirect map:

- An article is served at `/learn/{categoryPath}/s/{slug}` — the discriminant is `s`, not the `p` the README documents.
- No locale segment is ever generated. `parseUrl` accepts `/learn/fr/...` at request time, but every blog route is `dynamic = 'force-static'`, so unlisted paths 404. The `/learn/fr/blockchain/p/...` forms of the README do not exist on the live site; the three French articles are served under the same unmarked shape as the English ones.

Counting the surfaces the v1 build really produces:

- 11 article pages.
- 7 category pages, from the hardcoded list in `robusta-categories.ts`: blockchain, blockchain/ethers-js, blockchain/solidity, web, javascript, javascript/typescript, javascript/react. Three of them — blockchain/ethers-js, blockchain/solidity, javascript/react — hold zero articles, because `getPostsByCategory` requires an exact category-depth match. Two categories that do hold articles, privacy and theory, are absent from the list and therefore have no category page at all.
- 1 blog home, `/learn`.
- 0 blog roll pagination pages: `rollSize` is 12 and there are 11 articles, so `Math.ceil(11/12) = 1` and `/learn/page/{n}` generates nothing. The story's Definition of done asks for roll-page redirects; there are none to write.
- 14 tag pages, `/learn/tag/{tag}`, emitted verbatim from the frontmatter with no slugification — so `/learn/tag/DeFi` carries its capitals, and the redirect rule has to be case-exact.

That is roughly 33 generated URLs, plus 13 raw markdown files under `apps/robusta/public/learn` that Next.js serves as static assets at `/learn/**/*.md`. Those 13 files are keyed by file name, use a different frontmatter schema (`lang` and `category` instead of `locale` and `categoryPath` — see `public/learn/theory/solid-principles.md`), and pre-date the current slug scheme. They are a Gatsby-era dump, not a picture of what is indexed.

Two more facts the story does not record:

- The frontmatter cover images are broken on v1 today. `validateFrontMatter` strips `./` and the `images` prefix and prepends a slash, so `./images/aave-small.png` becomes `/aave-small.png` — and no such file exists under `public/`. Checked for nine of the eleven cover images: all missing. The v2 site must resolve these paths, not reproduce the mangling.
- Of 73 image files under `content/blog`, 44 distinct paths are referenced by an article. The `security/` and `prompt/` folders hold images and no article at all.

The story's Gap 1, on `apps/robusta/public/images`, is already answered by the epic's decision of 2026-07-29: nothing under it travels. The rationale attached to that decision is wrong — it says the referenced images sit under `public/learn/**/images`, when they sit under `content/blog/**/images` — but the conclusion holds, and the directory holds 23 files rather than the 32 the epic states. This brainstorm treats the point as settled and does not re-raise it.

## Requirements

BR-PYRAMID-1 governs the destination addresses of this migration: a v2 URL states the kind of page it addresses, so the redirect map targets route shapes and never content lookups.

- R-MIGRATELEARN-1 — The corpus that travels is the 11 articles of `apps/robusta/content/blog`. The 5 articles existing only under `public/learn` are outside it.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-2 — An article carries on the v2 site the same slug it carries on the v1 site.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-3 — The v1 URL inventory of record is the set the v1 build generates, not the set `apps/robusta/README.md` documents.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-4 — Every URL the v1 build generates resolves after the migration, permanently, to a v2 URL. No such URL ends on a 404.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-5 — A v1 URL reaches its v2 page in one redirect. No chain.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-6 — An article keeps its title, its date, its author, its tags, its category path and its cover image.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-7 — An article's declared locale is the language the article is written in.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-8 — Two articles that are translations of each other each declare the other; an article with no counterpart declares none.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-9 — An article with no declared author takes the author of the site configuration.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-10 — A nested category path is preserved as a path, not flattened to its last segment.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-11 — Every image an article references is served by the v2 site, from a path the article resolves without rewriting.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-12 — A v1 category page holding no article has no v2 counterpart and redirects to the nearest ancestor category that holds articles, or to the blog home when there is none.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-13 — An indexed tag URL redirects to the blog home; the v2 site has no tag page.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-14 — The v1-to-v2 URL mapping is recorded as a document and stays auditable until `apps/robusta` is retired.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-15 — The migration does not alter the prose of an article.
- R-MIGRATELEARN-16 — Nothing under `apps/robusta/public/images` and no file under `apps/robusta/public/learn` travels to the v2 site.

## 1. Product Role

**Problème:** the story is read as "copy 11 markdown files", which is the smallest and least valuable part of it.

**Décision (autonome):** this deliverable is an address-continuity operation with a content move attached. Its unit of work is the redirect map — roughly 33 generated URLs plus 13 raw asset URLs — and its unit of failure is a search result that stops resolving. It is not a re-editing pass, not a CMS, not an import tool meant to run twice, and not the place where the v2 site's SEO quality is decided.

**Rationale:** copying 11 files is an afternoon; the redirect map is what makes the copy worth doing, and framing the deliverable around the map is what keeps the empty category pages, the tag URLs and the raw markdown assets from being forgotten.

---

## 2. Target Audience

**Problème:** who this is for is left implicit, and it drives how much machinery is justified.

**Décision (autonome):** three parties, in this order of priority — the search-engine crawler that holds the v1 addresses, the reader arriving on a v1 link from a search result or a backlink, and the publisher, who is a single author with eleven articles and no editorial workflow. A fourth party, the future author writing article twelve, is served by the authoring documentation the story already plans, not by the migration itself.

**Rationale:** at eleven articles there is no editorial team to serve and no volume to automate for; the only party with a hard requirement is the crawler, which will not re-ask a 404, and everything else follows from satisfying it.

---

## 3. Core Problem

**Problème:** name the thing that breaks if this story is skipped.

**Options considérées:**
- The content disappears — false: the markdown stays in git, and the v2 site could be given fresh articles.
- The addresses disappear — every indexed `/learn/...` URL points at a site that no longer serves it, and the accumulated indexation of eleven articles published between 2019 and 2022 is lost.

**Décision (autonome):** the core problem is address continuity across a site replacement that does not change the domain. Content transfer is a precondition for solving it, not the problem itself.

**Rationale:** the seo-url-scheme decision of 2026-07-29 removed the only escape hatch — "the domain won't switch" means the old and new URLs live on the same host, so every unredirected v1 path becomes a 404 on a live domain rather than on a domain nobody visits.

---

## 4. Unique Value Proposition

**Problème:** why migrate rather than one of the two cheaper alternatives.

**Options considérées:**
- Keep v1 serving `/learn/*` alongside v2 — no redirects to write, but two Next.js apps share one domain forever, item 12 of the epic never closes, and the v1 code the epic calls unmaintainable stays in production.
- Start the v2 blog empty and let the v1 URLs die — zero work, and it discards seven years of published content and whatever indexation it holds.
- Migrate with a complete redirect map — the work described here.

**Décision (autonome):** migrate with a complete redirect map. What this deliverable uniquely provides is that the v2 site inherits the v1 site's indexation instead of starting from nothing, at the cost of one auditable mapping document.

**Rationale:** the first option defeats the epic's last item and keeps the code it exists to retire; the second throws away the only asset v1 has left.

## 5. Functional Scope

**Décision (autonome):** in scope —

- The 11 articles of `content/blog`, with their frontmatter, their prose unchanged, and their referenced images.
- Correcting the two articles whose declared locale contradicts the language they are written in.
- Declaring the two translation pairs.
- The redirect map covering every URL the v1 build generates: 11 articles, 7 category pages, the blog home, 14 tag pages.
- The redirect map covering the 8 raw `.md` asset URLs under `public/learn` whose article belongs to the corpus.
- The mapping document itself.

Out of scope —

- The 5 articles existing only under `public/learn`, and therefore their `.md` asset URLs: their fate is the editorial call the story's decision of 2026-07-29 sends elsewhere.
- Rewriting or updating any article's prose, however dated.
- The 29 image files under `content/blog` that no article references, including the whole `security/` and `prompt/` folders.
- Everything under `apps/robusta/public/images`, per the epic's decision.
- Sitemap, structured data, canonical and hreflang metadata, internal linking — seo-excellence, item 8.
- Deleting or retiring `apps/robusta` — item 12.
- Choosing the v2 route shapes, and choosing the content source — items 4 and 5, on which this one depends.

**Rationale:** the boundary is drawn at "what an indexed URL needs to keep resolving"; everything on the out list is either arbitrated elsewhere, editorial, or belongs to a story that owns it.

## 6. Core Features

### Feature: Corpus transfer

**Capability:** the 11 articles of `content/blog` are readable on the v2 site, each under its category path and in its locale, with title, date, author, tags and category preserved and its prose byte-identical.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Given the 11 corpus articles, When the v2 site is built, Then 11 article pages exist and their rendered prose is byte-identical to the markdown body of the source file.
- Given `javascript/typescript/completes-with.md` with `categoryPath: 'javascript/typescript'`, When its page is served, Then it is attached to the nested category `javascript/typescript` and not to `javascript`.
- Given `privacy/leaving-gmail.md`, which declares no author, When its page is served, Then it shows the author of the site configuration.
- Given an article file missing a mandatory field — date, title or category path — When the site is built, Then the build fails naming the file, rather than serving a partial page.

**Test Approach:** an automated build-time assertion comparing the produced article set against a fixture list of the 11 expected slugs and category paths; one rendering test per locale to check the metadata fields reach the page.

---

### Feature: Frontmatter correction

**Capability:** two data defects in the source tree are corrected as the articles move — the declared locale of `yield-farming.md` and `why-migration-gatsby-next.md`, both `fr` on articles written in English.

**Décision (autonome):** correct both to `en` during the migration, and carry every other frontmatter field through unchanged, including the ones the story's preserve list omits (`featured`, `published`, `public`).

**Rationale:** the correction is free — neither title contains a character whose slugification depends on the locale, so both slugs are unchanged and no v1 URL moves because of it — while leaving it in place would make the v2 site declare an English article as French, which seo-excellence's language and alternate criteria cannot be met with. Carrying the remaining fields verbatim is the least destructive default: a field the v2 site ignores costs nothing, and dropping one is a decision nobody asked for.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Given `yield-farming.md` and `why-migration-gatsby-next.md`, When they are migrated, Then their declared locale is `en` and their slug is identical to the one v1 derives.
- Given the corrected locale, When the v2 URL of each is computed, Then it carries no locale marker, since `en` is the default locale.
- Given any corpus article, When it is migrated, Then every frontmatter key present in the source is present in the target, save the two corrected locale values.

**Test Approach:** a unit test asserting that v1's `immutableSlugify` returns the same value for each of the 11 titles under both the declared and the corrected locale, run once as a regression guard; a diff of the source and target frontmatter key sets.

---

### Feature: Image transfer

**Capability:** every image an article references is served by the v2 site, and the cover image declared in frontmatter resolves — which it does not on v1 today.

**Décision (autonome):** carry the 44 referenced image paths, keep the article-relative reference form (`./images/...`) as the authoring contract, and resolve it at build time instead of rewriting it into the markdown. The unreferenced files stay behind with `apps/robusta`.

**Rationale:** relative references are what the 11 articles already contain and what an author will keep writing, so preserving the form means the corpus needs no edit at all; rewriting 44 paths into absolute URLs would be a prose change this story forbids itself. Leaving the unreferenced files behind loses nothing — `apps/robusta` is not deleted until item 12, and git holds them regardless.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Given an article referencing `./images/aave.png`, When its page is served, Then the image loads from the v2 site.
- Given `styled-components.md`, whose cover image is `../images/styled-logo.png` in the shared `content/blog/images` folder rather than a category folder, When its page is served, Then the cover image loads.
- Given any migrated article, When its page is served, Then no image request returns a 404.

**Test Approach:** a build-time check that every image path referenced by the corpus resolves to a file that ships, failing the build otherwise; a crawl of the built site asserting zero broken image responses.

---

### Feature: Redirect map

**Capability:** every URL the v1 build generates, plus the raw `.md` asset URLs of the corpus articles, resolves permanently to a v2 URL in a single hop.

**Décision (autonome):** the map is generated from v1's own route generation — the article set from `generateStaticParams`, the category set from `robusta-categories.ts`, the tag set from `getAllTags` — and never hand-written. The five classes of source URL map as follows: an article to its v2 article URL; a category page holding articles to its v2 category page; a category page holding none to its nearest ancestor category that holds articles; the blog home to the v2 blog home; a tag page to the v2 blog home, per the story's decision of 2026-07-29.

**Rationale:** hand-writing the map guarantees a divergence, because the slugs come from titles through a function nobody reads by eye and the README already documents a discriminant the code does not use. Sending empty category pages to their ancestor rather than to the blog home keeps the reader in the subject they clicked on and costs nothing: all three — blockchain/ethers-js, blockchain/solidity, javascript/react — have a populated ancestor.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Given `/learn/blockchain/s/ledger-versus-metamask`, When it is requested on the v2 site, Then it responds with a permanent redirect to the v2 URL of that article, and that URL responds 200.
- Given `/learn/blockchain/solidity`, a category page that holds no article, When it is requested, Then it redirects permanently to the v2 category page of `blockchain`.
- Given `/learn/tag/DeFi`, When it is requested with its exact capitalisation, Then it redirects permanently to the v2 blog home.
- Given any URL of the generated v1 set, When it is requested, Then it reaches a 200 response in exactly one redirect — never two, never a 404.
- Given `/learn/theory`, a category page v1 never generated, When it is requested, Then no redirect claims to exist for it.

**Test Approach:** an automated test that reads the generated map, requests every source URL against the built v2 site, and asserts a single permanent hop to a 200; the source list is itself produced by running v1's route generation, so the test fails if the two drift.

---

### Feature: Translation pairing

**Capability:** the two articles that exist in both languages declare each other, and the link resolves to a v2 URL.

**Décision (autonome):** two pairs, and only two — `the-source-of-yield-farming-profits` with `provenance-des-rendements-du-yield-farming-dans-la-blockchain`, and `why-i-made-the-migration-from-gatsby-toward-nextjs` with `pourquoi-jai-migre-de-gatsby-vers-nextjs`. Each article names its counterpart in its own frontmatter, and the declaration is symmetric. The other seven articles declare nothing.

**Rationale:** the v1 `PostFrontMatter` interface already reserves `enVersion` and `frVersion` for exactly this and no article ever used them, so the link has to be authored here; both pairs share a publication date and a cover image, which is what identifies them as translations rather than as two articles on one subject. Declaring an alternate on the seven others is what seo-excellence explicitly refuses.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Given the migrated corpus, When the translation declarations are read, Then exactly four articles carry one, forming two symmetric pairs.
- Given an article declaring a counterpart, When its page is served, Then the link resolves to a v2 URL that responds 200.
- Given an article whose declared counterpart does not exist in the corpus, When the site is built, Then the build fails naming both files.

**Test Approach:** a build-time validation of symmetry and resolvability across the corpus, plus one rendering test per pair.

---

### Feature: URL mapping record

**Capability:** the v1-to-v2 mapping exists as a document a human can audit, next to the v2 site's README, and stays accurate until `apps/robusta` is retired.

**Décision (autonome):** the document is generated from the same source as the redirect map and committed, rather than maintained by hand. It records, per source URL, the target URL and the class of mapping applied.

**Rationale:** the story's Documentation updates already require it, and generating it from the map is the only way it does not rot the first time a slug is re-derived; item 12 needs it to verify nothing was left behind before deleting v1.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Given the built redirect map, When the mapping document is regenerated, Then it is identical to the committed one, or the check fails.
- Given the mapping document, When a reader looks up any v1 URL, Then they find its target and why it maps that way.

**Test Approach:** a regeneration-and-diff check in the same suite that verifies the redirects.

## 7. Critical Edge Cases

**Décision (autonome):** the following are handled explicitly, and each has an acceptance criterion above or a stated non-behaviour.

- The README lies about the article discriminant (`p` where the code emits `s`) and about locale segments (`/learn/fr/...`, which 404s today). Any map built from the README is wrong; the map is built from the code.
- Two categories hold articles but have no v1 category page — privacy and theory. They gain a v2 category page and no redirect, because there is no v1 URL to redirect.
- Three category pages hold no article — blockchain/ethers-js, blockchain/solidity, javascript/react. They have no v2 counterpart and redirect to their ancestor.
- Tag values are used raw in URLs, so `DeFi` and `state-of-the-art` are case- and hyphen-sensitive redirect sources.
- Roll pagination produces nothing on v1 (11 articles, roll size 12). The Definition of done's "roll page" clause has an empty subject; this is recorded rather than silently satisfied.
- Two articles change their declared language, which changes their v2 locale but not their slug, and therefore not their v1 URL.
- One cover image points outside its category folder (`../images/styled-logo.png`) and would break a naive per-category copy.
- The frontmatter cover images do not resolve on v1 today, so "no regression against v1" is a weaker bar than "no broken image"; the second is the one this story holds itself to.
- The 13 `.md` files under `public/learn` are servable URLs, not source files. Eight belong to corpus articles and are redirected; five belong to the editorial call and are left alone.
- `apps/robusta` keeps serving until item 12, so during the overlap the same domain must not answer both a v1 page and a v2 redirect for one URL. Ordering is a deployment concern, flagged here because it is a way to produce a redirect loop.

## 8. Non-Functional Constraints

**Décision (autonome):**

- Correctness over completeness: a redirect pointing at a 404 is worse than a missing redirect, because it costs the reader a hop and tells the crawler the target is broken. The map is verified end to end before it ships.
- Redirects are permanent and single-hop. A chain through an intermediate v1 shape is treated as a defect.
- Scale is small and stays small: 11 articles, 44 images, about 46 source URLs. Nothing here justifies an import pipeline, a database or a migration tool that runs more than once.
- The migration is idempotent and re-runnable from the source tree, because it will be run several times during development and once for real.
- No content is rewritten: the markdown bodies are carried, not transformed.
- Cost: no third-party service, no paid API. `packages/imagine` is not involved and must not be, per the repository rule on its scripts.
- Compliance and privacy: none engaged. BR-PYRAMID-2 in the epic's wording — no visitor-intent tracking — is satisfied by adding nothing.

**Rationale:** at this scale the only expensive mistake is a wrong address, so the whole non-functional budget goes into verifying the map rather than into throughput.

## 9. External Dependencies

**Décision (autonome):** the migration depends on four things inside the repository and effectively nothing outside it.

- seo-url-scheme (item 4) — the destination URLs, and therefore every target in the map. Blocking, and currently short of one answer (see Gap 1).
- content-source (item 5) — how an article reaches the site, and therefore where the 11 files land and how their images resolve. Blocking.
- bootstrap-robusta-build (item 3) — transitively, since neither of the two above can land without the workspace.
- v1's `immutableSlugify`, pinned to `slugify@1.6.6` — the slugs are its output, so the v2 site either calls the same pinned version or asserts equality against a recorded list. This is the one dependency that cannot be re-derived if lost.

Outside the repository: Vercel serves the redirects, and Google Search Console is the only place the true indexed-URL set can be read (see Gap 4). Neither is on the critical path for building the map.

**Rationale:** naming the slugify pin as a dependency rather than an implementation detail is what stops a future rewrite from silently changing eleven addresses.

## 10. Major Risks

**Décision (autonome):** four risks carried, with the mitigation each is granted.

- Building the map from the wrong inventory. Three documents describe the v1 URLs and all three are stale — the README on the discriminant and on locales, the epic and `root.archi.md` on the corpus location, `ROADMAP.md` on both. Mitigation: the map is generated by running v1's route generation, and R-MIGRATELEARN-3 states the inventory of record.
- A slug that moves. The slug is derived from the title, so any change to the title, the locale charmap or the slugify version silently relocates a published article. Mitigation: the eleven slugs are recorded as a fixture and asserted, and the locale correction was checked to leave both affected slugs unchanged before being decided.
- Redirecting into a shape that does not exist yet. The v2 article URL is not fully determined — see Gap 1 — so a map written today could target `/articles/articles/{slug}`. Mitigation: the map's targets are expressed against the route scheme, and the story is already blocked on item 4 for implementation.
- Silent partial success. Eleven articles is few enough that a missing one is invisible in a build log. Mitigation: build-time assertions on the expected article count, on image resolution and on translation symmetry, each failing the build rather than warning.

Not carried as risks: content loss (git holds everything, and `apps/robusta` is not deleted until item 12) and adoption (the publisher is the author of the story).
